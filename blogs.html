Fun with GPU

Short, engineer-first notes on GPUs and LLM inference—where batching, memory layout, and host/device transfers decide whether the hardware is doing real work.

I Built a C++ Backend So My GPU Would Stop Eating Air


A tour of WarpGroup-Backend: VRAM-aware bin packing, pinned-memory transfers, and how to cut wasted GPU work when batching variable-length LLM sequences — with code and a few sharp words for default padding. The article treats batching policy as a first-class design choice: padding-to-max length burns cycles on meaningless compute, while dynamic packing under a VRAM budget forces an explicit trade-off between occupancy and tail risk. It traces a path from PyBind11-driven queues through aligned chunking into a FlashAttention-friendly memory contract, using cudaHostAlloc pinned pathways so the GPU sees steady work instead of idle gaps waiting on the host. . . . . 3 Agents. 3 LLMs. 1 Aging GPU: Engineering Parallel Inference on Bare Metal


A build log for VRAM Conductor: running three different LLMs concurrently on a single 8GB GPU without OOM crashes. It works through NVML-seeded admission control, a layer-streaming engine that double-buffers cudaMemcpyAsync transfers across two CUDA streams, and a stateless KV-swap helper for clean model-switch eviction. The throughline is a hard constraint taken seriously: three agents, three models, one aging card, and a discipline of measuring wall-clock savings instead of assuming pinned memory alone would save the day. . . . . Production-grade Agentic Inference

A line of work on agentic LLM execution under real constraints: shared-prefix prefill, GPU sharing in orchestrated environments, and reproducible latency/throughput measurement.

Prefill Once, Fan Out: KV Snapshot Sharing for Multi-Agent LLM Pipelines


Part 1 of the series: a systems-engineering approach to KV cache reuse that proves computing a shared prompt once beats N parallel prefills every time. It frames multi-agent and DAG-shaped LLM workloads where each branch would otherwise re-read the same long document into KV at full cost, and explains how copy-on-fork style host snapshots with RoPE-aware continuation can move time-to-first-token off the critical path. The emphasis is on end-to-end measurables—wall clock, prefix reuse, and branch start latency—grounded in a pinned llama.cpp orchestration surface rather than speculative engine features that are not available to call. . . . . GPU Time-Slicing for Concurrent LLM Agents on Kubernetes


Part 2 of the series: stop trusting the pod status. An end-to-end framework for measuring the hidden microarchitectural costs and memory contention of GPU sharing among AI Agents in Kubernetes. It pairs a single-node K3s lab setup with synchronized inference-style GEMM work alongside a second GPU-heavy tenant, then reads latency through percentiles and jitter instead of a superficial “job completed” signal. The aim is a receipt-grade account of time-sliced GPUs: when co-scheduled agents collide on memory bandwidth and queueing, what happens to tail latency under load, and which measurement choices expose contention that averages hide. . . . . GPU-Resident Top-K for Agentic RAG: I Built a CUDA Kernel So My Retrieval Step Would Stop Bouncing Off the GPU


Part 3 of the series: a CUDA kernel that keeps the entire retrieval hop — similarity scoring, per-block Top-K, and the final merge — resident on the GPU. It targets the default agentic RAG pattern where every tool call ships a query embedding to the CPU, sorts candidates there, and ships results back, treating that PCIe round-trip as the real bottleneck rather than the model itself. Across a 45-configuration sweep on a GTX 1080, the GPU-resident path beats the CPU round-trip baseline by up to 8.6x, while the post stays candid about where a simple V1 selector still loses at large K. . . . . Persistent Latent Memory for Multi-Hop LLM Agents: How a 6G Handover Paper Closes the Agent Cold-Start


Part 4 of the series: multi-hop LLM agents currently hand off context as a string, discarding the receiver's KV cache and any partial computation the sender already built up. This post adapts Inductive Latent Context Persistence (ILCP) — originally a peer-reviewed 6G handover protocol — into ilcp-for-agents, compressing a sender's pooled hidden state through a β-VAE, transporting it across the hand-off, and projecting it into receiver-side memory tokens. V1 ships the architecture (PyTorch, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, gated MLP projector) with agent-side benchmarks explicitly left as future work, while the underlying 6G protocol's validated numbers are presented and clearly labelled as such. . . . . Perspectives on AI Infrastructure

Shorter, opinionated pieces on where AI memory and agent orchestration are headed — less code, more architecture and systems thinking about what today's approaches are actually standing in for.

RAG Was Always a Temporary Workaround. What is Next?


An argument that Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a high-overhead translation layer built around a temporary limitation, not the final architecture for AI memory. It traces why vector databases, chunking, and retrieval exist largely because models cannot yet persist their own internal neural state, and what changes once persistent latent-space memory matures. The piece stops short of dismissing RAG outright — vector search keeps its place at the boundary where machines explain themselves to other machines — while arguing that its role as the default conversational memory mechanism is a bridge, not a destination. . . . . When the Code Becomes the CEO: Why Your Next Manager Might Be a Decentralized Agentic Loop


A forward-looking piece on what happens when coordination work — the part of management that is really just routing, prioritization, and delegation — gets absorbed by a decentralized agentic loop. It frames the shift not as agents replacing people outright, but as a structural change in where decisions get made once orchestration itself becomes cheap and reliable enough to automate.
